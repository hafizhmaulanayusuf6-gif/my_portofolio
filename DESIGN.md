# Portfolio design direction

## Identity

Personal developer portfolio for hiring teams and potential collaborators. The tone is friendly, professional, personal, and editorial. The language is English, with concise copy grounded in verified profile and project information.

## Source direction

The owner specified a warm orange and neutral palette, Manrope typography, a two-column desktop hero with a real portrait, and purpose-built mobile layouts. The Google Stitch project is the intended visual source of truth, but no Stitch connection or export was available for this implementation. Use the Stitch screens if they are supplied later.

## Visual system

- Paper: `#FFFEFC`
- Soft apricot: `#FFF8F3`
- Ink: `#171717`
- Body text: `#626262`
- Border: `#EBE7E3`
- Accent: `#F36B21`
- Typeface: Manrope, with system sans-serif fallback
- Layout: centered content, left-aligned copy, asymmetric two-column hero on desktop, single-column portrait flow on mobile
- Shape: mostly square corners and thin borders, reserved for the portfolio's calm editorial tone
- Theme: light, based on the supplied warm palette
- Motion: short hover and active-state feedback, no scroll choreography

## Design read and dials

Reading this as: a personal developer portfolio for hiring teams and collaborators, with a warm editorial visual language, dial ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 3 / MOTION 1.

## Composition and purpose

- The hero pairs the developer's name and role with the supplied portrait so identity leads the page.
- About uses an actual personal photo and short biography from the public profile.
- Skills use verified technology names and monochrome brand marks so the logos identify tools without adding a second palette.
- Project cards group actual screenshots, descriptions, technology tags, and working source/demo links in one scannable unit.
- Experience is framed as study and project practice because no employment history was supplied.
- The five process markers are numbered because the owner specified a five-stage sequence.
- A narrow orange mark recurs on the portrait and active navigation to connect the page without spreading the accent across every control.
- Section compositions change to follow their content: portrait with narrative, technology list, project gallery, learning record, and ordered process.

## Content constraints

Use the project owner's verified profile and repository content. Keep missing employment, CV, LinkedIn, testimonials, and metrics out of the page. Do not reuse the old portfolio's visual design. Do not treat Google Stitch fidelity as verified until the approved screens are available for comparison.
